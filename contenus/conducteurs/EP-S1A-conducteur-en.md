### 43. titre 3.2

### 44. etapes
- Écran : Four scales, from least to most informative

  - *Ordinal*: also, rank
  - *Interval*: also, measure differences

- Voix : There are four scales. On a nominal scale, you can only say whether two values are equal or different, and count them. On an ordinal scale, you can also rank them. On an interval scale, you can also measure differences: temperature in degrees Celsius. On a ratio scale, you can also say twice as much: kilograms, euros, durations.

### 45. etapes
- Écran : Is 20 °C twice as hot as 10 °C?

- Note à l'écran : 0 °C = 273.15 K; zero kelvin is absolute zero.
- Voix : Let's compare. Twenty kilograms really is twice ten kilograms, because zero kilograms means zero mass. But zero degrees Celsius isn't the zero of temperature: it's the temperature at which water freezes, a reference point chosen by convention. On a scale whose zero truly means nothing, kelvins, going from ten to twenty degrees means going from two hundred and eighty-three to two hundred and ninety-three kelvins: about three and a half percent more, not double. That ratio doesn't describe how warm it feels: it only shows that twenty degrees isn't double ten.

### 50. titre 4
- Écran : Where do data come from?
- Voix : How many people are unemployed in France? The answer depends on who is counting, and how.

### 51. titre 4.1
- Écran : Four ways to collect data
- Voix : There are four main ways to produce data.

### 52. etapes
- Écran : The four main sources
  - *Census*: ask everyone
  - *Survey*: ask a sample
  - *Administrative records*: record to manage
  - *Sensors and platforms*: keep traces
- Voix : A census asks the whole population. A sample survey asks only a sample, chosen to represent it. Administrative records register people in order to run a service: taxes, births and deaths, registrations. Finally, sensors and platforms keep traces: cars going by, purchases, log-ins.

### 53. etapes
- Écran : One strength, one limitation
  - Census: *complete*, but heavy and costly
  - Survey: *fast*, but a margin of error
  - Records: *huge*, but defined by administrative rules
  - Sensors: *continuous*, but only where installed
- Voix : Each source has a strength and a limitation. A census is complete, but heavy and costly to run. A survey is fast, but it has a margin of error, and some people don't answer. Administrative records are huge and already available, but they only count what their rules ask them to count. Sensors measure continuously, but only where they're installed. Platforms, for their part, only see their own users.

### 54. texte
- Écran : What *isn't collected* doesn't show up in the data
- Voix : Remember the general idea: every way of collecting data leaves its mark on the number. What isn't collected simply doesn't show up in the data.

### 55. titre 4.2
- Écran : Unemployment: survey or register?
- Voix : Back to unemployment, which is measured in two different ways.

### 56. etapes
- Écran : Two ways of counting
  - Insee: a *survey*, ILO definition
  - No job, *available*, *actively* looking
  - France Travail: a *register* of jobseekers
- Voix : On one side, Insee, the French statistics office, surveys a sample of households and applies the definition of the International Labour Organization. Under that definition, a person is unemployed if they have no job, are available to work, and are actively looking for one. On the other side, France Travail, the public employment service, keeps a register: it counts the people who have signed up, under its own rules.

### 57. chiffre

- Chiffre clé : 2.7
- Note à l'écran : Insee, Labour Force Survey: 2,677,000 people, seasonally adjusted.
- Voix : According to Insee's survey, France had two point seven million unemployed people under the ILO definition in the second quarter of twenty twenty-six.

### 58. texte
- Écran : A registered jobseeker isn't always *ILO-unemployed*, and vice versa
- Note à l'écran : Insee: 3.3 million people registered with France Travail in category A, Q2 2026 (France excluding Mayotte).
- Voix : The number of people registered with France Travail is different: three point three million in category A, in the same quarter. That's not a mistake: the two figures don't measure the same thing. Someone who worked even one hour in the reference week isn't unemployed under the ILO definition, even if they stay registered. And someone actively looking for work without registering can be counted by the survey, but not by the register.

### 68. etapes
- Écran : Key takeaways
  - Data are *measurements*, under a definition
  - Who it's about: *population, unit, variable*
  - Label, rank or quantity: the *scale* says what you can compute
  - Every source has a *strength* and a *limit*
  - Which *question*? Which *definition*? *Who* measured it, when and how?
- Voix : What to remember. One: a statistical question expects answers that vary, and a data point is always a measurement, made by someone, under a definition. Two: before comparing, ask who the number is about, the population, the unit, the variable; changing the unit changes the number. Three: a number can be a label, a rank or a quantity, and the scale tells you which operations make sense. Four: census, survey, records or sensors, each source has a strength and a limit. In front of any number, ask three questions: which question, which definition, and who measured it, when and how?
