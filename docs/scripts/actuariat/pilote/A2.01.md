## Description EN (YouTube)

Insuring one phone is a gamble: the insurer pays either nothing or
\$500. Insuring 10,000 phones is almost predictable. Why? In this
episode of our actuarial science series, we treat the cost of a policy
as a random variable, compute its expected value and standard deviation,
and see how pooling shrinks the uncertainty per policy: sigma divided by
the square root of n. We also see what pooling cannot do: it makes
losses more predictable, not automatically profitable. Worked exercise
at the end. Fictional insurer and figures, checked calculations.

## Calculs recalculés par la machine

- Expected cost of one phone: `0.98 × 0 + 0.02 × 500` = 10
- Variance of the cost of one phone: `0.02 × 500^2 - 10^2` = 4900
- Standard deviation of one phone: `sqrt(0.02 × 500^2 - 10^2)` = 70
- Same result: 500 × √(p(1 − p)): `500 × sqrt(0.02 × 0.98)` = 70
- Standard deviation divided by the mean: `70 / 10` = 7
- Expected thefts, 100 phones: `100 × 0.02` = 2
- Standard deviation of thefts, 100 phones: `sqrt(100 × 0.02 × 0.98)` =
  1.4
- Expected thefts, 10,000 phones: `10000 × 0.02` = 200
- Standard deviation of thefts, 10,000 phones:
  `sqrt(10000 × 0.02 × 0.98)` = 14
- Relative spread, 100 phones, in %:
  `100 × sqrt(100 × 0.02 × 0.98) / (100 × 0.02)` = 70
- Relative spread, 10,000 phones, in %:
  `100 × sqrt(10000 × 0.02 × 0.98) / (10000 × 0.02)` = 7
- Std. dev. of average cost, 100 phones: `70 / sqrt(100)` = 7
- Std. dev. of average cost, 10,000 phones: `70 / sqrt(10000)` = 0.7
- P(3 thefts or more out of 100), in %:
  `100 × (1 - sum(comb(100, k) × 0.02^k × 0.98^(100 - k) for k in range(3)))`
  = 32
- P(221 thefts or more out of 10,000), in %:
  `100 × (1 - sum(exp(log(comb(10000, k)) + k × log(0.02) + (10000 - k) × log(0.98)) for k in range(221)))`
  = 7.3
- Premiums collected, 100 phones: `100 × 11` = 1100
- Fewest thefts costing more than the premiums, 100 phones:
  `floor(100 × 11 / 500) + 1` = 3
- Fewest thefts costing more than the premiums, 10,000 phones:
  `floor(10000 × 11 / 500) + 1` = 221
- Expected thefts, 2,500 phones: `2500 × 0.02` = 50
- Square root of 2,500: `sqrt(2500)` = 50
- Std. dev. of average cost, 2,500 phones: `70 / sqrt(2500)` = 1.4
