## Description EN (YouTube)

Would you rather have \$1,000 today or \$1,000 in 30 years? Same number,
very different value. In this episode of our actuarial science series,
we build the most basic tool of the actuary: compound interest and
discounting. We see why \$1,000 due in 30 years is worth about \$231
today at 5% a year, why the interest rate matters so much to an insurer,
and how to do it all in a spreadsheet. Worked exercise at the end.
Fictional rates, checked calculations, no investment advice.

## Calculs recalculés par la machine

- Simple interest per year: `0.05 × 1000` = 50
- Simple interest over 30 years: `30 × 0.05 × 1000` = 1500
- Total with simple interest: `1000 × (1 + 0.05 × 30)` = 2500
- Balance after 1 year: `1000 × 1.05` = 1050
- Balance after 2 years: `1000 × 1.05^2` = 1102.5
- Future value after 30 years at 5%: `1000 × 1.05^30` = 4322
- Balance after 10 years: `1000 × 1.05^10` = 1629
- Balance after 20 years: `1000 × 1.05^20` = 2653
- Rule of 72 at 5%: `72 / 5` = 14.4
- Exact doubling time at 5%: `log(2) / log(1.05)` = 14.2
- Present value of \$1,000 in 30 years at 5%: `1000 / 1.05^30` = 231.38
- Check: \$231.38 grows back to \$1,000: `231.38 × 1.05^30` = 1000
- Present value, 10 years: `1000 / 1.05^10` = 614
- Present value, 20 years: `1000 / 1.05^20` = 377
- Present value at 2%: `1000 / 1.02^30` = 552
- Present value at 5%: `1000 / 1.05^30` = 231
- Present value at 8%: `1000 / 1.08^30` = 99
- Ratio of present values, 2% vs 5%:
  `(1000 / 1.02^30) / (1000 / 1.05^30)` = 2.4
- 1.04 to the power 10: `1.04^10` = 1.4802
- Present value of \$10,000 in 10 years at 4%: `10000 / 1.04^10` =
  6755.64
- Check: `6755.64 × 1.04^10` = 10000
