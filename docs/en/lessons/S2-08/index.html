S2-08

Why square the deviations? Variance and standard deviation

Two bus lines both make you wait 5 minutes on average. On one, you always wait about 5 minutes; on the other, sometimes 1, sometimes 9. How do you capture that difference in a single number? We compute the variance and standard deviation by hand, see why the deviations are squared, and what "a standard deviation of 3 minutes" means. Worked exercise at the end. Fictional examples, checked calculations. Series "Reading numbers without being fooled".

Videos · Video in preparation

Learning objectives

  • Compute the variance and standard deviation of four values by hand
  • Explain why deviations from the mean sum to zero, and why we square them
  • Interpret a standard deviation in the units of the data

Prerequisites

  • S2.07: measuring spread, simply
  • S2.04: what the mean really says

Why square the deviations?

To measure spread, statisticians square the deviations. Why not just add them up?

Two lines, same mean

Let's start with two bus lines.

Four waits on each line (in minutes)

We timed four waits on each line. On line one: four, five, five and six minutes. On line two: one, three, seven and nine minutes. Both lines have the same mean, five minutes, but the second is far less regular.

  • Line 1: 4, 5, 5, 6
  • Line 2: 1, 3, 7, 9
  • Mean: 5 min for both

Line 2: deviations from the mean

Let's measure how far each wait is from the mean, on line two. One minus five, minus four; three minus five, minus two. Seven minus five, plus two; nine minus five, plus four. Added together, these deviations come to zero: as we saw, that is always the case.

  • 1 − 5 = −4 and 3 − 5 = −2
  • 7 − 5 = +2 and 9 − 5 = +4
  • Sum of deviations: 0

The deviations cancel out: their sum measures nothing

Positive and negative deviations cancel out. The deviations from the mean always add up to zero: so their sum cannot measure spread. We need to get rid of the signs.

The variance

The idea: square each deviation.

Line 2: deviations squared

A square is always positive. Minus four squared, sixteen; minus two squared, four. Two squared, four; four squared, sixteen. They add up to forty, and the mean of these squares, forty divided by four, is ten: that is the variance.

  • (−4)² = 16 and (−2)² = 4
  • 2² = 4 and 4² = 16
  • Mean of the squares: 40 ÷ 4 = 10

Variance: the mean of the squared deviations

As a formula: for each value, take its deviation from the mean, square it, add everything up, then divide by n. The Greek letter sigma squared stands for the variance.

\sigma^2=\dfrac{1}{n}\times\sum_{i=1}^{n}(x_i-\bar{x})^2

The standard deviation

There is still a problem with the units.

Back to minutes: the square root

The variance is in minutes squared, a unit that is hard to interpret directly. So we take its square root: that is the standard deviation. For line two, the square root of ten, about three point one six minutes. For line one, whose variance is zero point five, about zero point seven one minutes.

\sigma=\sqrt{10}\approx 3.16\ \text{min}

Standard deviation: the typical size of deviations from the mean

The standard deviation is read in the units of the data. On line two, a wait typically differs from the mean by about three minutes; on line one, by less than a minute. It isn't exactly the average deviation, but it gives its order of magnitude.

Divide by n, or by n − 1?

You will sometimes see the sum of squares divided by n minus one instead of n. To estimate the variance of a population from a sample, n minus one is often used. We'll see why in a later lesson.

Exercise

Standard deviation of 2, 4, 4, 6: dividing by n, then by n − 1?

Your turn. Work out the standard deviation of two, four, four and six. First dividing by n, then dividing by n minus one.

Solution

Let's work it out. The mean is four; the squared deviations are four, zero, zero and four, eight in total. Dividing by n, the variance is two, and the standard deviation is the square root of two, about one point four one. Dividing by n minus one, the variance is eight thirds, and the standard deviation about one point six three. The two versions give about one point four one and one point six three.

  • Mean 4; squared deviations 4, 0, 0, 4: sum 8
  • By n: 8 ÷ 4 = 2, standard deviation √2 ≈ 1.41
  • By n − 1: 8 ÷ 3 ≈ 2.67, standard deviation ≈ 1.63
Answer : ≈ 1.41 (by n); ≈ 1.63 (by n − 1)

Key points

Key points. Deviations from the mean cancel out: we square them. The variance is the mean of those squares. And the standard deviation, its square root, is read in the units of the data.

  • Deviations from the mean cancel out: square them
  • Variance = mean of the squared deviations
  • Standard deviation = square root of the variance, in the data's units
