How many people live in Paris? What counts as data

How many people live in Paris: 2.1 million, 11 million or 13.3 million? All three figures are correct, because each answers a different definition of "Paris". First lesson of the course: what makes a question statistical, and why a data point is always a measurement, made by someone, under a definition. Source: Insee, 2023 population census (commune, urban unit and functional area of Paris). Series "Reading numbers without being fooled". CHALLENGE

2.1, 11 or 13.3 million: which one is right?

Pick your answer before we go on. Which of these three figures is the right one?

A statistical question

To answer, let's start with a simpler question: what is a statistical question?

Two questions, two kinds

Let's compare two questions. How old is my neighbour today? There is a single answer: you just ask her. How old are the people on my street? This time, the answers vary from one person to the next.

  • "How old is my neighbour?": a single answer
  • "How old are the people on my street?": answers that vary

A statistical question expects answers that vary

That's the key difference. A statistical question is about a group, and it expects answers that vary. Statistics are there to describe and understand that variability.

Data are measurements

Second idea: data don't fall from the sky.

Behind every data point

Behind every data point, there are three things. A precise question. A definition of what is being counted. And someone who measures, at a given date, with a method.

  • A precise question
  • A definition of what is counted
  • Someone who measures, at a given date

How many people live in Paris?

Let's go back to Paris, with the census figures from Insee, the French statistics office.

Three definitions of "Paris"

Insee publishes three figures, for three definitions of Paris. The city of Paris, within its official boundaries: two point one million people. The built-up area, meaning Paris and the surrounding towns that form one continuous urban area with it: eleven million. The functional area, which adds the surrounding towns where at least fifteen percent of workers commute to the Paris core: thirteen point three million.

  • The city of Paris: 2.1 million
  • The built-up area: 11.0 million
  • The functional area, adding towns where at least 15% of workers commute to the core: 13.3 million

Insee, 2023 census: 2,103,778, 11,013,774 and 13,320,752 people.

The answer to the challenge: all three figures are right. Depending on the definition, the number of people is multiplied by more than six.

A number isn't enough on its own: it depends on the question asked

So a figure isn't enough on its own. It depends on the question asked and on the definition chosen. Before comparing two figures, check that they answer the same question.

Exercise

A: the height of the Eiffel Tower. B: how long Parisians take to get to work. Which one is a statistical question?

Your turn. Which of these two questions is a statistical question? A: how tall is the Eiffel Tower? B: how long do Parisians take to get to work?

Solution

Question A has only one answer: you measure it once. Question B is about thousands of people, and the times vary from one to the next. So B is the statistical question.

  • A: a single value, measured once
  • B: times that vary from person to person
Answer : B is a statistical question

Three questions to ask any number

Remember three questions to ask any number. Which question does it answer? Under which definition? And who measured it, and when?

  • Which question?
  • Which definition?
  • Who measured it, and when?
