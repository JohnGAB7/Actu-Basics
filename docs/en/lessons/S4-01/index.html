S4-01

Three readings of a probability

"Seventy percent chance of rain tomorrow": what does it really mean? With a balanced die, a long series of "seventy-percent" days, and an imaginary expert, we meet three readings of the same probability: symmetry, long-run frequency, degree of confidence. And we translate them into fractions, decimals and percentages. Worked exercise at the end. Fictional data. Series "Reading numbers without being fooled".

Learning objectives

  • Tell apart three readings of a probability: symmetry, long-run frequency, degree of confidence
  • Write a probability as a fraction, a decimal and a percentage

Prerequisites

  • S1.08: percentages and points

'70% chance of rain tomorrow': what does that really mean?

The news says: seventy percent chance of rain tomorrow. It does not rain. Was the forecast wrong? Not necessarily.

Reading no. 1: symmetry

First reading, the simplest: symmetry.

When all cases are equivalent

A balanced die has six faces. We make the assumption — a strong one — that each has the same chance of coming up. Then the probability of a six is one sixth: about sixteen point seven percent. This is the symmetry reading: it works when you can list a finite number of equivalent cases.

  • A balanced die: six faces, equally possible
  • The probability of a 6 is 1/6
  • Decimal: 0.167 · Percentage: 16.7%

Reading no. 2: long-run frequency

Second reading: the frequency observed over a long series.

When we observe many times

Take all the days when the forecast announced seventy percent. Over enough comparable announcements — many hundreds, many thousands — it should rain about seven times in ten. We then call the forecast calibrated. For one isolated day, 'seventy percent' doesn't mean the computer is half sure, and a frequency computed on a handful of days settles nothing.

  • Take every day forecast at 70%
  • Count how often it rained
  • Over enough comparable forecasts, about 7 in 10 if the forecast is calibrated

The frequency stabilises as n grows

In an imaginary simulation, the frequency wobbles at first, and settles around seventy percent after a few thousand days.

Imaginary simulation: the frequency gets close to 70% as more days are observed.

Reading no. 3: degree of confidence

Third reading: degree of confidence.

When there is only one try

An imaginary expert says: I am eighty percent sure my hypothesis is right. She has not counted symmetric cases, and she has not repeated her experiment a thousand times. Her eighty percent is a degree of confidence: the bet she would make if asked, revisable when new data arrive.

  • An imaginary expert: 80% confident in her hypothesis
  • It is her rational bet
  • Revisable when new data arrive

Fraction, decimal, percentage

Three ways to write the same probability.

The same probability, three writings

One sixth, zero point one six seven, sixteen point seven percent. One quarter, zero point two five, twenty-five percent. Three tenths, zero point three, thirty percent. Seven tenths, zero point seven, seventy percent. Always: decimal times one hundred equals percentage. And the decimal stays between zero and one.

  • 1/6 · 0.167 · 16.7%
  • 1/4 · 0.25 · 25%
  • 3/10 · 0.3 · 30%
  • 7/10 · 0.7 · 70%

A classic mistake

A classic mistake.

A 70% forecast is not wrong if it does not rain

The news announces seventy percent rain. It does not rain. Was the forecast wrong? No: if thirty percent of seventy-percent days are dry, that is as expected. Calibration can only be judged on enough comparable announcements: a single day neither confirms nor refutes it.

  • 70% means 70 times in 100 in the long run
  • A dry day does not contradict it
  • It is contradicted only if, across all 70% days, rain falls 30% of the time

Exercise

For each of the four sentences, say which reading: symmetry, frequency, or confidence.

Four sentences. For each, say which reading is at work: symmetry, frequency, or confidence. One: 'the probability of a three on a balanced die is one in six'. Two: 'out of one thousand tosses, heads came up about 497 times'. Three: 'I am sixty percent sure the meeting will take place'. Four: 'across all draws, a lottery pays out one time in twenty'. Pause.

Solution

Sentence one, symmetry: the six faces are all equivalent. Sentence two, frequency: a long series has been observed. Sentence three, confidence: a one-off event, a rational bet. Sentence four, frequency across all draws, or symmetry if we assume each outcome is equally likely: the two readings coincide when the draw is fair.

  • Sentence 1: symmetry (equivalent faces)
  • Sentence 2: frequency (a long series of trials)
  • Sentence 3: confidence (rational bet on a one-off)
  • Sentence 4: frequency or symmetry, depending on the reading
Answer : Symmetry · frequency · confidence · frequency (or symmetry)

Key points

Key points. A probability can be read three ways: through symmetry of cases, through observed frequency in the long run, or as a degree of confidence in a rational bet. It is written as a fraction, a decimal between zero and one, or a percentage. And a seventy-percent announcement is not refuted by a dry day.

  • A probability, three readings: symmetry, frequency, confidence
  • Three writings: fraction, decimal, percentage
  • A 70% forecast is not wrong on a dry day; it is calibrated in the long run
