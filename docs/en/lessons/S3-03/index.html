S3-03

Better in every group, worse overall: Simpson's paradox

One treatment works better than another for patients with small kidney stones, and better for those with large ones too. Yet overall, the other treatment seems to win. How is that possible? Using the figures from a 1986 observational medical study, we reproduce Simpson's paradox and see the role of a confounding variable. Source: C. R. Charig et al., British Medical Journal, 1986. Worked exercise (fictional hospitals). Series "Reading numbers without being fooled".

Videos · Video in preparation

Learning objectives

  • Reproduce a Simpson's paradox from a table
  • Explain the role of a confounding variable when groups are pooled
  • Know that neither the total nor the detail is enough without understanding how the groups were formed

Prerequisites

  • S3.02: cross-tabs and conditional percentages

Better in every group, worse overall?

Can a treatment be better for mild cases, better for severe cases, and yet worse overall? Yes. And it happened in a real medical study.

Two treatments for kidney stones

In nineteen eighty-six, a British team looked back at patients treated in different ways for kidney stones.

Overall, B looks better

Let's look at two of them, each used on three hundred and fifty patients. Treatment A, open surgery, succeeded two hundred and seventy-three times: seventy-eight percent. Treatment B, a less invasive percutaneous technique, succeeded two hundred and eighty-nine times: about eighty-three percent.

  • Treatment A, open surgery: 273 successes out of 350, 78%
  • Treatment B, percutaneous technique: 289 out of 350, ≈ 83%

Observational study: patients were not randomly assigned to the treatments.

By stone size, A wins twice

Now let's split the patients by the size of their stone. For small stones, A succeeds ninety-three percent of the time, B eighty-seven percent. For large stones, A succeeds seventy-three percent of the time, B sixty-nine percent. A wins in both groups.

  • Small stones: A 93% (81 of 87); B 87% (234 of 270)
  • Large stones: A 73% (192 of 263); B 69% (55 of 80)

Who is in each group

How can the total say the opposite of every group?

Who got which treatment?

It all comes down to who got which treatment. Open surgery was mostly given to patients with large stones, the hardest cases. The percutaneous technique mostly treated small stones, the easiest cases. So the total mixes groups of very different sizes, and B benefits from its easier patients.

  • A: 263 large stones out of 350, the hard cases
  • B: 270 small stones out of 350, the easy cases
  • The total mixes groups of different sizes

Stone size is a confounding variable

Stone size is linked to both at once: to the treatment received, and to the chance of success. It's a confounding variable, which contributes here to the reversal. Forget it, and it can reverse the conclusion: that's Simpson's paradox. And since patients weren't randomly assigned, other differences between the groups remain possible.

Neither the total nor the detail is enough alone

Should you always trust the detailed figure? Not necessarily. Here it is more telling, because stone size existed before the treatment. But if you split by a consequence of the treatment, the detail could mislead in turn. Neither the total nor the detail is enough without understanding how the groups were formed.

Exercise

Hospital 1: mild 90/100 survive, severe 300/400. Hospital 2: mild 340/400, severe 70/100. Which does better, at equal severity?

Your turn, with two imaginary hospitals. At hospital one, ninety mild patients out of a hundred survive, and three hundred severe patients out of four hundred. At hospital two, three hundred and forty mild patients out of four hundred, and seventy severe out of a hundred. Work out the rates by group and overall. Which hospital has the better survival rates, at equal severity?

Solution

Let's see. Among mild patients, hospital one does better, ninety percent against eighty-five; among severe patients too, seventy-five against seventy. Overall, though, hospital one shows seventy-eight percent, and hospital two eighty-two. The reason: hospital one mostly gets severe cases. In this example, hospital one has the higher survival rate in both groups, though that alone doesn't prove it gives better care.

  • Mild: H1 90%, H2 85%. Severe: H1 75%, H2 70%
  • Overall: H1 390/500 = 78%, H2 410/500 = 82%
  • H1 mostly gets severe cases
Answer : Hospital 1, in every group

Key points

Key points. A total can say the opposite of each of its groups. The cause: a confounding variable, and groups of unequal size. So when you see a comparison, always ask who is in each group.

  • A total can say the opposite of every group
  • The cause: a confounding variable and groups of unequal size
  • Always ask: who is in each group?
