What does a correlation of 0.8 really measure?

The correlation coefficient r runs from −1 to 1. But what exactly does it measure? We compute it by hand on four points using z-scores, then discover Anscombe's quartet: four very different clouds, one and the same correlation. Plus two mistakes to avoid: reading r = 0 as "no relationship", and reading r as a slope. Worked exercise at the end. Source: F. J. Anscombe, The American Statistician, 1973. Series "Reading numbers without being fooled".

What does r = 0.8 really measure?

A study reports a correlation of zero point eight between two quantities. Is that a lot? And above all, what exactly does this number measure?

A number between −1 and 1

The correlation coefficient is written r.

r sums up how lined up a cloud is

It always lies between minus one and one. An r of one: every point sits exactly on a line going up. An r of minus one: they sit exactly on a line going down. An r of zero: no linear association in this data. And zero point eight: a clear upward trend, with points scattered around the line.

  • r = 1: points on a line going up
  • r = −1: on a line going down
  • r = 0: no linear association in this data
  • r = 0.8: clear trend, points scattered around it

The calculation on four points

Let's compute r by hand, on a tiny cloud.

Four points, means 2.5 and 4

Here are four points: one and two, two and three, three and five, four and six. The mean of the x values is two point five, the mean of the y values is four.

[Chart for this step]

Made-up points. Standard deviations computed by dividing by n, as in the standard deviation episode.

Turn every value into a z-score

Step one: replace every value by its z-score, the gap from the mean divided by the standard deviation. The standard deviation of x is about one point one two, that of y about one point five eight. Small x and y values get negative scores, large ones positive scores.

z_x:\ -1.34\quad -0.45\quad 0.45\quad 1.34

r = mean of the products of z-scores

Step two: for each point, multiply its x z-score by its y z-score, then take the mean. One point seven, zero point two eight, zero point two eight, one point seven: the mean is about zero point nine nine. The points are almost lined up.

r=\dfrac{1.70+0.28+0.28+1.70}{4}\approx 0.99

Top right, bottom left: r goes up

Here's the intuition. A point at the top right has two positive scores, a point at the bottom left two negative scores: either way the product is positive and r goes up. Points at the top left or bottom right give negative products and pull it down.

Anscombe's quartet

In nineteen seventy-three, the statistician Francis Anscombe published four small series of eleven points.

Series 1: r ≈ 0.82

Series one: a cloud going up, with points scattered around a line. Its correlation coefficient is about zero point eight two. Nothing surprising.

One r, four stories

Four clouds, one correlation, and four different stories. A line with noise. A regular curve. A line and one outlier. One point doing all the work. Anscombe's lesson: before you read r, always look at the cloud.

  • A line with noise
  • A regular curve
  • A line and one outlier
  • One point doing all the work

Two classic mistakes

Two traps remain that people often fall into.

r = 0, and yet a perfect relationship

Trap one: thinking that r equals zero means no relationship. Here, y is exactly the square of x: the relationship is perfect. But it isn't a straight line, and the products of the z-scores cancel out: r is zero. A zero r only says there is no linear trend.

[Chart for this step]

Made-up points: y is the square of x.

r is not a slope

Trap two: reading r as a slope. If y equals x, or y equals ten times x, the points are perfectly lined up and r is one in both cases. r only says how well the points line up. It's the slope of the line, not r, that says by how much y changes when x goes up.

  • y = x and y = 10 × x: r = 1 in both cases
  • r says whether the points are lined up
  • The slope says by how much y changes

Exercise

Match A, B, C, D, E to: −0.9; −0.4; 0; 0.5; 0.95

Your turn. Match each of the five clouds to one of these values of r: minus zero point nine, minus zero point four, zero, zero point five, and zero point nine five.

Solution

Here's the answer. A, tight and rising: zero point nine five. B, rising but scattered: zero point five. C, a symmetric U: zero, because there is a relationship but it isn't linear. D, tight and falling: minus zero point nine. E, falling and scattered: minus zero point four. The sign gives the direction, the size gives how lined up the points are.

  • A: 0.95, tight and rising
  • B: 0.5, rising but scattered
  • C: 0, a symmetric U, non-linear relationship
  • D: −0.9, tight and falling
  • E: −0.4, falling and scattered
Answer : A 0.95; B 0.5; C 0; D −0.9; E −0.4

Key points

Key points. r runs from minus one to one: its sign gives the direction, its size the alignment. It's the mean of the products of the z-scores. It measures the strength and direction of a linear association: always look at the cloud. And it isn't a slope.

  • r runs from −1 to 1: direction and alignment
  • r = mean of the products of z-scores
  • r measures a linear association: look at the cloud
  • r is not a slope
