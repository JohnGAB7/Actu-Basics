S3-04

Do two quantities move together? The scatter plot

Every point is one individual, and the shape of the cloud tells a story. With fifteen imaginary countries, wealth and life expectancy, we learn to read a scatter plot by asking four questions: form, direction, strength, outliers. And we see how a logarithmic scale can straighten out a curve. Worked exercise at the end. Fictional data. Series "Reading numbers without being fooled".

⏱ 2 min read✎ Written by JG↻ Updated 2026-11-09
Videos · Video in preparation

Learning objectives

  • Build and read a scatter plot
  • Describe a relationship by its form, direction, strength and outliers
  • Get an intuition for how a logarithmic scale changes the shape of the cloud

Prerequisites

  • S2.03: the shape of a distribution

Do two quantities move together?

Do people in richer countries live longer? To see whether two quantities move together, there is a very simple chart.

One point per individual

The idea fits in one sentence.

Wealth and life expectancy, 15 imaginary countries

Each country becomes a point: its wealth per person across, its life expectancy up the side. Here are fifteen imaginary countries, placed one by one. The cloud climbs fast at first, then flattens out.

Imaginary countries: numbers made up for the example.

Four questions to ask

To describe a cloud, always ask four questions.

Describe a cloud in four words

Here are the four questions. Form: do the points follow a straight line, a curve, or separate clusters? Direction: when one goes up, does the other go up or down? Strength: are the points tight around their trend, or widely scattered? Outliers: does any point stand clearly apart from the rest?

  • Form: a line, a curve, clusters?
  • Direction: going up, going down?
  • Strength: are the points tight?
  • Outliers: does a point stand apart?

One country stands apart

Let's apply them to our countries. The form is a curve, not a straight line. The direction is upward. The strength is fairly high: most points stay close to the curve. But one country clearly stands apart: rich, and yet a life expectancy of only sixty-three years. That's the one to go and understand. And this cloud shows an association: it doesn't prove that wealth alone makes people live longer.

Changing the scale

What if we changed the way the horizontal axis is marked?

Logarithmic scale: the curve straightens

On this axis, each step doesn't add: it multiplies wealth by ten. One thousand dollars, ten thousand, one hundred thousand. With this logarithmic scale, the same points line up almost along a straight line. The cloud hasn't changed, only the scale has: always check how an axis is marked.

Logarithmic scale: each step multiplies GDP by 10. Same imaginary countries.

Hours of revision and marks, 14 pupils

Here is one last cloud: fourteen imaginary pupils, their hours of revision and their marks. Take a good look at it.

Fictional pupils.

Exercise

Describe this cloud: form, direction, strength, outliers.

Your turn. Describe this cloud by answering the four questions: form, direction, strength, and outliers.

Solution

Here is one answer. The form is roughly a straight line. The direction is upward: more hours of revision go with better marks. The strength is fairly high: the points stay close to the line. And one pupil stands apart: nine hours of revision, and five out of twenty. Perhaps they were ill on the day of the test: once again, an outlier is a question to ask.

  • Form: roughly a straight line
  • Direction: upward, more hours, better marks
  • Strength: fairly tight
  • Outlier: one pupil, 9 hours of revision and 5 out of 20
Answer : Straight, upward, tight, one outlier

Key points

Key points. In a scatter plot, each point is an individual, placed according to two variables. Describe it by its form, direction, strength and outliers. And always check the scale of the axes.

  • One point per individual, two variables
  • Describe: form, direction, strength, outliers
  • Check the scale of the axes
