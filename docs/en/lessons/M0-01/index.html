3 out of 12 is what percent? Fractions and proportions

3 people out of 12 cycle to work: what percentage is that? Without a calculator, you simplify the fraction, switch to a decimal, then to a percentage. We also learn not to confuse a "1 to 3" ratio with a "1 in 4" fraction, and how to scale a quantity in proportion. Fictional examples, checked calculations. Series "Reading numbers without being fooled", toolbox.

Learning objectives

  • Simplify a fraction
  • Convert a fraction to a decimal, then to a percentage
  • Tell a ratio (part to part) from a fraction (part of the whole)
  • Use proportional reasoning (the rule of three)

Prerequisites

  • None: basic arithmetic refresher

3 out of 12: what percent?

In a team of twelve people, three cycle to work. What percentage is that? No calculator.

CHALLENGE

3 out of 12: 20%, 25% or 30%?

Pick one before we go on: twenty, twenty-five or thirty percent?

Simplifying a fraction

Step one: simplify the fraction.

Divide top and bottom by the same number

Three out of twelve. Look for a number that divides both three and twelve exactly: three. Three divided by three is one. Twelve divided by three is four. Three out of twelve is one out of four: one person in four.

\[\dfrac{3}{12}=\dfrac{3\div 3}{12\div 3}=\dfrac{1}{4}\]

The fraction changes its form, not its value

Simplifying doesn't change the amount. Three out of twelve and one out of four describe exactly the same share of the group, just written with smaller numbers.

Fraction, decimal, percentage

Step two: from the fraction to a percentage.

Three ways to write the same share

One out of four is one divided by four: zero point two five. And zero point two five is twenty-five hundredths, so twenty-five percent.

\[\dfrac{1}{4}=0.25=\dfrac{25}{100}=25\%\]

of the team cycle to work

25%

The answer to the challenge: three out of twelve is twenty-five percent.

A few useful fractions to remember

A few benchmarks save a lot of work. One in two, fifty percent. One in four, twenty-five percent. One in five, twenty percent. One in ten, ten percent.

  • 1 in 2 = 0.5 = 50%
  • 1 in 4 = 0.25 = 25%
  • 1 in 5 = 0.2 = 20%
  • 1 in 10 = 0.1 = 10%

Ratio or fraction?

Watch out for a common trap: the ratio.

In the team: 3 cycle to work, 9 don't

In the team, three people cycle and nine get to work some other way. A ratio compares one part with another part: three to nine, one cyclist for every three non-cyclists. A fraction compares a part with the whole: three out of twelve, one in four.

  • Ratio: 3 to 9, so 1 to 3
  • Fraction: 3 out of 12, so 1 in 4

"1 to 3" does not mean one third

To go from the ratio to the whole, add the two parts: one plus three, four. So reading one to three as one third would be a mistake: the share of cyclists is one in four, twenty-five percent, not thirty-three. Before you calculate, always ask: is this one part compared with another part, or a part compared with the whole?

Scaling in proportion

One last tool: scaling a quantity when things are proportional, sometimes called the rule of three.

A recipe for 4, scaled for 6

A recipe calls for three hundred grams of flour for four people. How much for six? First, go back to one person: three hundred divided by four, seventy-five grams. Then multiply by six: four hundred fifty grams.

\[4\ \mathrm{people}\ \rightarrow\ 300\ \mathrm{g}\]

Only if it's proportional

This only works if the two quantities are proportional: twice as many people, twice as much flour. The time it takes to preheat the oven doesn't change with the number of guests.

Exercise

Out of 40 students, 6 have a bus pass. What percentage?

Your turn. In a group of forty students, six have a bus pass. What percentage is that, without a calculator?

Solution

Six out of forty: divide top and bottom by two, three out of twenty. To get one hundred at the bottom, multiply by five: fifteen out of one hundred. Fifteen percent.

  • 6 out of 40 = 3 out of 20 (divide by 2)
  • 3 out of 20 = 15 out of 100 (multiply by 5)
Answer : 15%

Key takeaways

Key takeaways. To simplify, divide top and bottom by the same number. Go from the fraction to a decimal, then to a percentage. A ratio compares two parts; a fraction compares a part with the whole. And scaling only works when things are proportional.

  • Simplify: divide top and bottom
  • Fraction → decimal → percentage
  • Ratio = part / part; fraction = part / whole
  • Scaling: only when it's proportional
