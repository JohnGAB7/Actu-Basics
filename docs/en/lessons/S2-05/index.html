S2-05

Mean or median?

Why do most people earn less than the "average salary"? Nine customers sit in a café, then a tenth, very rich one walks in: the mean shoots up, the median barely moves. Learn how to find a median, predict the gap between the two, and pick the right one for your question. Made-up example, all numbers checked. Series "Reading numbers without being fooled".

⏱ 5 min read✎ Written by JG↻ Updated 2026-11-03
Videos · Video in preparation

Learning objectives

  • Find the median of an odd and of an even number of values
  • Predict which way the mean moves away from the median in a skewed distribution
  • Choose the summary that answers a given question

Prerequisites

  • S2.04: computing a mean
  • S2.03: the shape of a distribution

Why do most people earn less than the average salary?

Why do most people earn less than the average salary? It sounds impossible. And yet it happens all the time.

CHALLENGE

In a group, what share of people earn less than the mean?

Here's a question to start. In a group of workers, what share earn less than the average salary? Less than half, exactly half, or more than half? Hold on to your answer.

The mean

Let's start with the mean.

Nine customers in a café earn between €1,600 and €2,800 a month

Nine customers are sitting in a café. They earn between one thousand six hundred and two thousand eight hundred euros a month.

Their mean salary

The mean is the total divided by the number of people. Together they earn nineteen thousand eight hundred euros. Divide by nine, and you get two thousand two hundred euros.

\[\mathrm{mean}=\dfrac{\mathrm{total}}{\mathrm{count}}\]

A tenth customer walks in. He earns €200,000 a month.

The door opens. A tenth customer walks in, and he earns two hundred thousand euros a month.

The mean shoots up

Let's do the sum again. Nineteen thousand eight hundred plus two hundred thousand, divided by ten. The café's mean salary jumps to twenty-one thousand nine hundred eighty euros.

\[\dfrac{19{,}800+200{,}000}{10}\]

of the customers now earn less than the mean

90%

Nine customers out of ten now earn less than the mean. Nobody's pay has changed. One extreme value has dragged the mean upward.

The median

There's another summary that holds up much better: the median.

Finding the median

Finding the median takes three steps. First, put the values in order, from smallest to largest. With an odd number of values, the median is the one in the middle. With an even number, you take the average of the two middle values.

  • Put the values in order
  • Odd number of values: take the middle one
  • Even number: average the two in the middle

Where is the middle?

To find the middle, take n plus one, divided by two, where n is the number of values. With nine values, that's the fifth one. With ten values, you land on five and a half: the median sits halfway between the fifth and sixth values.

\[\mathrm{position}=\dfrac{n+1}{2}\]

The café's median

Back to the café. Before the rich customer arrives, the fifth value is two thousand two hundred euros, and that's the median. Afterwards, we average the fifth and sixth values, two thousand two hundred and two thousand three hundred: two thousand two hundred fifty euros.

  • Before: the 5th value, €2,200
  • After: (2,200 + 2,300) ÷ 2 = €2,250

One extreme value

Compare the two. One extreme value pushes the mean from two thousand two hundred to almost twenty-two thousand euros. The median goes up by just fifty euros.

  • Mean: from €2,200 to €21,980
  • Median: from €2,200 to €2,250

The ten salaries, lowest to highest

Let's line up the ten salaries, from lowest to highest: nine stay clustered together, and the median stays with them, at two thousand two hundred fifty euros. The tenth, two hundred thousand euros, single-handedly drags the mean up to twenty-one thousand nine hundred eighty euros, above nine salaries out of ten.

Nine salaries clustered at the bottom, a single one at the top.

Mean and median depend on the shape of the distribution

This is a general rule, and it depends on the shape of the distribution. When a few very large values form a long tail toward the top, as salaries and wealth often do, the mean ends up above the median. When the distribution is symmetric, the two are close. And when the tail points toward low values, the mean falls below the median.

  • Long tail toward high values: mean above the median
  • Symmetric distribution: mean close to the median
  • Long tail toward low values: mean below the median

Mean above the median: at least half of the values are below the mean

That answers the challenge. By definition, at least half of the values are less than or equal to the median. So if the mean is above the median, at least half of the values are below the mean. For salaries, that means at least half of workers, and often many more.

Exercise

Seven salaries: €1,500, 1,600, 1,700, 1,800, 1,900, 2,000, 2,100. Mean and median, before and after a €20,000 salary joins?

Your turn. Seven monthly salaries: one thousand five hundred, one thousand six hundred, one thousand seven hundred, one thousand eight hundred, one thousand nine hundred, two thousand, and two thousand one hundred euros. Work out the mean and the median. Then do it again after a salary of twenty thousand euros joins the group.

Solution

Here's the solution. Before, the total is twelve thousand six hundred euros. Divided by seven, the mean is one thousand eight hundred euros, the same as the median, the fourth value. Afterwards, the total is thirty-two thousand six hundred euros, divided by eight: four thousand seventy-five euros. With eight values, the median is the average of the fourth and fifth: one thousand eight hundred fifty euros. The mean has more than doubled, while the median gained only fifty euros.

  • Before: 12,600 ÷ 7 = €1,800; median €1,800
  • After: 32,600 ÷ 8 = €4,075
  • Median: (1,800 + 1,900) ÷ 2 = €1,850
Answer : The mean more than doubles, the median gains €50

Which one should you use?

The mean is sensitive to extreme values: a single very large value can pull it upwards. The median, by contrast, is determined by the middle position in the ordered data. That does not make it universally better: the right measure depends on the question we want to answer.

Each summary answers its own question

Each summary answers its own question. To know what a typical worker earns, the median is the right answer. To know what all the salaries cost together, you need the mean, multiplied by the number of workers. The median can't tell you that. The most honest choice is often to report both, because the gap between them tells you on its own whether the distribution is skewed.

  • "What does a typical worker earn?" → the median
  • "What do all the salaries cost?" → the mean, × headcount
  • The most honest choice: report both

The median has a blind spot: it ignores how big the gaps are

The median has a blind spot too: it ignores how big the gaps are. Whether the rich customer earns two hundred thousand euros or two million, the café's median stays the same. It doesn't see inequality.

Key takeaways

Let's recap. One: for the median, sort the values, then take the middle. Two: an extreme value pulls the mean, and barely moves the median. Three: choose the summary that fits your question, and usually report both.

  • Median: sort, then take the middle
  • One extreme value pulls the mean, barely the median
  • Choose by question, and report both
